**What the sequence change does**

The variant deletes 16 nucleotides (c.339_354) in exon 3 of CASQ2. Because 16 is not a multiple of 3, the reading frame shifts after codon 113. One submitter (Labcorp) gives the full protein change as p.Ser113Argfs*6, meaning a premature stop codon about five residues downstream. This is a frameshift, not a missense, stop-gain, or in-frame change. A transcript with a premature stop this early is expected to be degraded by nonsense-mediated decay (NMD), so little or no CASQ2 protein would be made. That is a prediction. I did not find evidence in this record that a truncated protein is present in patient tissue, so I do not describe one as confirmed.

**Findings from the record**

1. **Notation differs between sources, but it is the same variant.** ClinVar lists NM_001232.4 and the older NM_001232.3 for the same change. The protein change appears as p.Ser113fs (ClinVar and the assignment), p.Ser113Argfs*6 (Labcorp), and G112+5X (older literature name). I kept the assigned notation.
2. **Condition labels are inconsistent.** Submitters list CPVT 1, CPVT 2, "cardiovascular phenotype," or "not provided." I recorded this without resolving it.
3. **No classification conflict.** All five submissions are Pathogenic, and the aggregate is Pathogenic. The OMIM entry (2006) does not contribute to the aggregate, and the newest submission (Ambry, 2026) shows the record is still being updated. This reflects the record on the access date, and classifications can change.
4. **Functional evidence is limited.** The main experimental study is di Barletta 2006, in which the mutant was expressed in rat cardiac myocytes. According to the OMIM entry for it, the mutant did not bind calcium, cells had lower sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium content, and delayed afterdepolarizations appeared after isoproterenol. Most other submissions rely on clinical testing and on the expected effect of loss-of-function variants. This is a small experimental base from an expressed mutant, not from patient muscle.
5. **Inheritance caveat.** ClinGen lists CASQ2 as associated with an autosomal recessive phenotype. The reported cases involve the deletion in the homozygous state or together with a second CASQ2 variant. The OMIM comment states that heterozygous carriers in the original family had no ventricular arrhythmias, so the Pathogenic label applies mainly to disease in people with variants in both copies.
